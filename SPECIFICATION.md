ORVEX Specification v0.1
1. Overview
ORVEX (Open Reasoning & Verifiable Evaluation Framework) is an open framework for evaluating arguments, reasoning, rebuttals, and debate performance through transparent and reproducible criteria.
ORVEX is designed to be independent of any single AI model, platform, or evaluator.
2. Core Principle
An ORVEX evaluation should be based on the quality of the reasoning and argumentation presented, rather than the identity, popularity, or reputation of the participant.
3. Evaluation Areas
ORVEX evaluates debate performance across several core areas:
Argumentation — The quality, relevance, and development of arguments.
Reasoning — The logical connection between claims, premises, and conclusions.
Evidence — The quality and appropriate use of factual support.
Rebuttal — The ability to identify and respond to opposing arguments.
Relevance — Whether the speech directly addresses the motion and debate.
Consistency — Whether the position remains logically consistent throughout the debate.
Clarity — How clearly and understandably the reasoning is communicated.
Comparative Impact — The relative strength of the arguments compared with the opposing side.
4. Evaluation Independence
ORVEX should be implementable by different evaluators, including:
Human judges
Different AI systems
Independent software implementations
Debate platforms
Different implementations may use different technologies, but they should follow the same publicly defined ORVEX principles.
5. Transparency
ORVEX evaluations should be explainable.
An evaluator should be able to identify:
What criteria were evaluated
How the criteria affected the result
Why one argument was considered stronger or weaker
How the final evaluation was reached
6. Scope
ORVEX is intended for the evaluation of structured arguments and debates.
The framework may later support different debate formats, scoring systems, judging methods, and specialized evaluation modules.
7. Versioning
This document represents ORVEX Specification v0.1.
The specification may be revised as the framework develops.
Future versions should document significant changes to the evaluation methodology.
